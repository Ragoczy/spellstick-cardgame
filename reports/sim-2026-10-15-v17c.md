# Rules v0.17, phase c: fouls and the referee

Plan: `PLAN.md`, "Phase c" (decisions 1 and 10). Phase b rules, where a dirty play is an ordinary
reaction after the dice, against the same rules with fouls declared before the dice and a referee,
on the same 5,000 seeds. Every number has a 95% interval; the full tables follow.

## What changed

- A dirty play is a **foul, declared before the dice** (attacker first, then defender). It uses
  the side's one reaction spell for the contest.
- **The referee:** if the two dice match after rerolls, every side that fouled has its player
  **sent off** for the game. The spot stays empty until a Substitute; a ball they held goes to
  the opposing player. A caught foul still hurts if its side won.
- The computer fouls when its chance of winning times the hurt beats the 1-in-6 send-off risk,
  and uses rerolls to break a double it's on or chase one the other side is on.

## Notes

- **The computer fouls more than before** (1.39 a game, against 0.98 in v0.16 and 0.57 in phase b).
  A dirty play has no other use, so it now treats one as a cheap card, and declaring before the
  dice means it no longer waits for a contest it already knows it won. About one player in four
  or five games is sent off.
- Spells cast is back above v0.16 because of the extra fouls.
- Hurts and real injuries are still under target. Phase c2 (physical attacks) and phase e are
  the planned levers.
- Under the old timing (online matches started before v0.17), dirty plays are still reactions
  after the dice, with no referee.

# Comparison: Phase c, 2026-10-15

Made by `npm run sim-compare`: 5000 computer-vs-computer games per run (seed block 1), 2 lanes, card set 0.16-prototype. Took 151 s on 8 workers.

- Before: config changes `foulTiming=reaction`; cards data/cards.prototype.json.
- After (Phase c): config changes `foulTiming=before`; cards data/cards.prototype.json.

Every result has a 95% interval: if the same run were repeated with new seeds, the result would land in that range 19 times out of 20. Both runs play the same seeds (the same deals and first player), and each change is measured seed by seed, so a small real change still shows. A target is "Yes" when the whole interval is inside it, "No" when the whole interval is outside it, and "Borderline" when luck could put it either side.
