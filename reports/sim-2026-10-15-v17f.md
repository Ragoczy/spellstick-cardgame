# Rules v0.17, phase f: player costs and the team cap

Plan: `PLAN.md`, "Phase f". Every named player gets a **cost**, and a built team's players may cost
at most the **team cap**. Costs come only from measured values, never from how often a skill is
used.

## What each part is worth

Measured by `npm run measure-value` (`value-2026-10-15.md`): one team gets the change and plays an
unchanged team. Each of 5,000 seeds is played twice, sides swapped, for 10,000 games per change.
The value per point is the edge in points per 100 games, divided by the cards that got the change.

| Part | Per point, per card (95% interval) | Used in cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speed | +0.575 (+0.535 to +0.615) | yes |
| Shot | +0.559 (+0.519 to +0.598) | yes |
| Defense | +0.510 (+0.470 to +0.550) | yes |
| Faceoff | +0.121 (+0.080 to +0.161) | yes |
| Toughness | +0.036 (+0.003 to +0.069) | yes |
| Save (goalies) | +6.61 (+6.21 to +7.02) | yes |
| A Resonant | +0.077 (+0.041 to +0.112) | yes |
| Quick (+2 Speed when evading) | +0.491 (+0.451 to +0.532) | yes |
| Heavy hands | −0.045 (−0.081 to −0.009) | **counts 0** |
| Sturdy | −0.083 (−0.119 to −0.048) | **counts 0** |

## The formula

A player's cost is the base plus the scale times what they're worth above the weakest value of
each part in the pool. For example, Speed 5 is 2 points above the pool's lowest Speed of 3. The
cheapest field player costs 6, and the average field player and the average goalie both cost 10.
`npm run price-players` writes the values to `data/player-values.json` and the costs into the card
data. The tests check every cost against the formula.

Claude: costs count from the pool's weakest values, not from zero. The measurements say what one
*more* point is worth. Counting from zero would price a goalie at about 70 next to a field
player's 10.

| | Costs |
| --- | --- |
| Field players | 6 to 15, average 10.0 (most 8 to 12) |
| By type | Runner 11.1, Striker 10.6, All-rounder 10.4, Playmaker 10.2, Anchor 9.0, Stopper 8.6 |
| Goalies | Save 5: 4. Save 6: 25 to 26. |
| Starter set | 218 |
| **Team cap** | **240** (an average 24-player team) |

The goalie gap is what the measurement says: one point of Save on both goalies is worth about as
much as one point of Speed on all 22 field players.

## Roster targets

From `npm run roster-targets` (`roster-targets-2026-10-15.md`): 20 team pairs × 250 seeds × 2
sides, 10,000 games each, with the cap off so a dearer team can exceed it.

| Target | Result | Wanted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10% cost gap: the dearer team scores | 62.0% (61.1 to 62.9) | 55–65% ✓ |
| Defense-heavy vs Striker-heavy at equal cost: Defense-heavy scores | 40.5% (39.6 to 41.4) | 45–55% ✗ |

## What needs Paul

1. **Defense-heavy teams are overpriced.** At equal cost, a team of Stoppers and Anchors loses
   about 3 games in 5 to Strikers and Runners. The costs add stats up in a straight line. Defense
   is worth less once a team already has plenty, and a defense-heavy team is short of Speed and
   Shot. Fixes, cheapest first:
   - price each stat by type: measure +1 on just the types that lean on it;
   - or give Stoppers and Anchors a flat discount, tuned until this target is met.

   Either is one more measuring run.
2. **Being Shaken is worse than being Injured** when the team has a field player in hand. An
   Injured player is swapped at once for a fresh player, for free. A Shaken player stays on the
   field, unable to act, at −2. So Sturdy (which turns Injured into Shaken) hurts its owner, Heavy
   hands helps the victim, and Toughness is worth almost nothing. Both abilities cost 0 for now.
   This is a game-feel question. Possible fixes: a forced substitution costs something (for
   example, the sub comes on Shaken), or a Shaken player can be swapped out for free.
